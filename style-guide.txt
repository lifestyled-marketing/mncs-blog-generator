# Global Writing Style Guide

These rules apply to every brand, on top of the brand's messaging guide. A
brand's messaging guide defines voice and tone. This file defines rules that
apply regardless of voice. If a brand guide conflicts with a rule here on
anything these rules cover, the rules below win.

## Banned rhetorical patterns

Avoid the "It's not X. It's Y." reframe pattern and similar formulaic
rhetorical pivots (e.g. "We don't just X. We Y." or "This isn't about X.
It's about Y."). These read as AI-generated. Use direct, plain statements
instead.

Examples of what to avoid:
- "It's not about the data. It's about the story."
- "We don't just build tools. We build relationships."
- "This isn't a blog post. It's a playbook."

Rewrite as direct statements:
- "The story matters more than the data."
- "We build relationships, not just tools."
- "This is a playbook."

## Punctuation

Do not use em dashes in any response. Use commas, periods, or parentheses
instead.

## Links and citations (no invented URLs)

Never fabricate URLs. This applies to every link in the post, internal and
external. A previous post shipped with a list of links to blog posts that did
not exist on the brand's website. That cannot happen again.

External links and citations:
- Only link to a URL that appears verbatim in the verified research provided
  to this writing step (Phase 1 and Phase 2 output for EC, the RESEARCH NOTES
  block for MN). If you did not see the URL in the research, you do not have
  it.
- When citing a stat or claim, name the source organization and the year
  inline. If a verified URL was provided in the research, you may render the
  source as a Markdown link `[source name](url)`. If no URL was provided, name
  the source in plain text. Do not guess the URL of a study, news article, or
  organization page.
- Do not link to a domain you have not been given. No "according to a Stanford
  study" linking to a fabricated `stanford.edu/...` URL.

Internal links (links to other pages on the brand's own website):
- You do not know what content exists on the brand's website. You have not
  been given a sitemap. Do not invent slugs. Do not guess URLs. Do not link
  to paths like `/blog/anxiety-tips`, `/services/emdr`, or anything similar.
- When an internal link would help the reader, leave a placeholder for a
  human to resolve later. Use this exact format with no URL:
  `[INTERNAL LINK SUGGESTION: anchor text | topic: short description of what
  the linked page should be about]`
- Examples:
  - Good: `[INTERNAL LINK SUGGESTION: read more about EMDR phases | topic:
    detailed walkthrough of EMDR's 8 phases]`
  - Bad: `[link to /blog/emdr-phases]`
  - Bad: `[read more about EMDR phases](/blog/emdr-phases)`

If you are not certain a link is real, do not include it. A missing link is
fine. A made-up link is a bug.
