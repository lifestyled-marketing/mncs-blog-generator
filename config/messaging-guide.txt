Mary Noone Campaign Strategy is a consulting agency for progressive local and state candidates who want a modern plan and someone who will actually say the quiet part out loud. Mary brings corporate-level problem solving and real campaign experience, so she can diagnose fast, cut distractions, and set priorities that hold up under pressure. She helps you run an integrated campaign, including digital, that turns your goals into decisions, and decisions into votes.
TAGLINE
Clear Strategy, Bold Vision, Campaigns That Build Political Movements
MISSION / GOAL
Help progressive local, state, and down ballot candidates win by turning data, messaging, and digital into a clear, modern, disciplined campaign strategy that works in tandem with a robust field strategy.

Why Choose Us?
Mary Noone is a strategist, not a cheerleader. She brings decades of experience across corporate and political work, plus deep training, and she uses it to diagnose problems fast and build the best possible solution for your race. She is on the cutting edge of what is working now, with a strong network and a clear point of view about how campaigns win today. As an outside voice, she spots gaps your team will miss, brings focus to the chaos, and gives you action-ready guidance you can actually implement. You keep ownership and execution, Mary makes sure every move ladders up to your goals while also building a bench of new progressive operatives. 
Enemies
* The old campaign playbook.  Running the same tactics because “that’s how it’s always done,” instead of using what works now.

* Digital denial.  Treating digital as optional, separate, or secondary, instead of part of the campaign’s core operating system.

Audience
Progressive candidates running for state legislature and down ballot, including county and city races. Often leaders in their community, credible, values-driven, and coachable, but not yet strong campaign operators. They want strategic direction and accountability, not someone to take over their whole campaign.  


PAIN
* They are overwhelmed by advice, vendors, and tactics that do not add up to a plan.
* They feel pressure to “be everywhere” and still worry they are missing what matters.
* They lack a clear view of what is working in today’s digital ecosystem.
* They do not know how to prioritize with limited time, staff, and budget.
* They suspect their campaign has blind spots, but cannot see them from inside the bubble.
DESIRE
* A clear path to victory, with priorities they can defend and execute.
* Straight answers about what is not working and what to do next.
* A modern playbook that integrates message, field, fundraising, and digital.
* Confidence and control, so they can lead their team instead of reacting all day.
* Effort that turns support into votes.

Lexicon
The Mary Noone Campaign Strategy voice is smart, direct, and grounded. It should sound like a strategist who can talk about data and systems, but chooses plain language because candidates need decisions, not theory. The tone is coaching-forward and action-first. It should feel like, “Here’s what’s true, here’s what matters, here’s what we do next.”
WHAT WE USE
* Clear verbs, fewer abstract nouns.
* “You” language speaks to the candidate’s choices and constraints.
* Keep it practical, prioritize priorities, tradeoffs, timelines, and next steps.
* Be candid without being harsh, direct without being cynical.
* Treat digital as integrated, not as a separate department.


CORE PHRASES
* Truth and clarity
   * “I’m going to tell you what I see.”
   * “Here’s the gap, and here’s how we close it.”
   * “If we do not change this, we will not hit your goal.”
   * “Let’s be honest about what is working and what is not.”
   * “This is the hard call, and it’s the right one.”
* Strategy and priorities
   * “Your goals decide your plan.”
   * “Pick the few moves that win the race.”
   * “What are we saying no to this week?”
   * “We’re not adding tactics, we’re building a system.”
   * “Make the plan simple enough to run.”
* Digital as part of the engine
   * “Digital is part of the field, not a side project.”
   * “Your campaign is an ecosystem, everything connects.”
   * “Message discipline applies online too.”
   * “Content is not the goal, votes are.”
   * “We build a digital plan that supports turnout and persuasion.”
* Execution and accountability
   * “Decisions, then calendar.”
   * “Turn strategy into weekly actions.”
   * “Protect your time, protect your message.”
   * “If it doesn’t move votes, it’s not a priority.”
   * “We measure what matters, then adjust fast.”
* Coaching posture
   * “You run the campaign, I help you run it smarter.”
   * “I’m here to coach, challenge, and keep you on track.”
   * “You’ll leave each session with clear next steps.”
   * “I’m not taking over, I’m sharpening your leadership.”
   * “We build the plan together, you own the execution.”
WHAT WE AVOID   
* Overused buzzwords.  “synergy,” “omnichannel,” “disruption,” “growth hacking,” “move the needle.”  
* Vague promises.  “We’ll amplify your voice,” “We’ll increase engagement,” “We’ll build awareness.”  
* Performative political phrasing that does not connect to voters’ lives.
* Jargon-heavy messaging that signals “insider” instead of “real person.”  
* Any tone that sounds smug, scolding, or chronically online. 
* Magical thinking. “We’ll go viral,” “One perfect message,” “This one trick.”

Story Blocks
Campaigns get stuck when they follow the old playbook, do busywork, and treat digital like a separate department. These Story Blocks are the alternative: clear truth-telling, hard priorities, integrated digital and field, disciplined execution, and coaching that builds real operators.
1 – TRUTH & CLARITY
* Proof point: Candidates do better when someone names the real gap fast, without sugarcoating. The work starts with, “Here’s what’s true, here’s what matters,” then a hard recommendation tied to the goal. 
* Anecdote: In 1992, a sign in Bill Clinton’s Little Rock campaign HQ captured the whole discipline: “It’s the economy, stupid.” It was blunt, internal, and it kept staff focused on what would decide votes instead of chasing every shiny issue. That is the exact type of “say it plainly so we can act” moment this brand talks about.
2 – EXECUTION & ACCOUNTABILITY
* Proof point: Strategy only matters if it turns into weekly actions. You make decisions, put them on the calendar, protect time and message, measure what matters, then adjust fast. If it does not move votes, it is not a priority.
* Anecdote: Obama’s 2012 team treated digital like a performance system. Amelia Showalter (digital analytics) has described extensive A/B testing of subject lines, asks, message drafts, and even formatting, sometimes testing many variations before sending. Their email testing program became a major fundraising driver, and “Hey” is often cited as a top-performing style of subject line because it felt human, not political. This is the model for “measure, learn, repeat” discipline.
3 – DIGITAL IS PART OF THE ENGINE
* Proof point: Digital is not optional or separate. It is part of the field operation and the campaign’s operating system, and it has to support persuasion and turnout with the same message discipline you use everywhere else.
* Anecdote: During Zohran Mamdani’s 2025 NYC mayoral run, the campaign built a Manychat Instagram chatbot that turned casual engagement into real voter action. By using comment-to-DM triggers and automated replies, the system drove over 45,000 clicks from Instagram DMs and helped turn online excitement into an actual volunteer army. Rather than just posting for likes, the campaign used social tools to pull supporters into actionable behavior like signing up to canvass, volunteer, and engage offline.

Brand vs. Personal Voice
Mary Noone Campaign Strategy (MN)
________________


Overview


Mary’s messaging uses two clear voices that work together. The company voice exists to help candidates win through strategy, clarity, and execution. The personal voice exists to show Mary as a real person with values and conviction, delivered with a “Likeable Badass” style, meaning warm, direct, and confident at the same time.


QUICK RULE FOR CHOOSING
* If the question is about winning, messaging, or execution, lead with company voice.
* If the question is about beliefs, values, or Mary’s stance, use a personal voice, and label it clearly.
* If it’s a heated moment, start with company voice for structure, then add a short personal voice line only if it helps, and only if you label it.

If you want, I can also add a short “Do / Don’t” list under each voice (one screen long) so it’s easy to use while writing posts, podcast answers, and debate prep.


________________




Mary Noone Campaign Strategy
TALKS ABOUT
   * Campaign strategy and execution (planning, timelines, accountability, weekly action)
   * Messaging and persuasion (how to communicate with voters, what to say, how to say it)
   * Digital as part of the overall campaign engine (not “just posting,” but integrated communications)
   * Positioning and differentiation (why a candidate, why now, why them)
   * Process and infrastructure (launch readiness, coalition building, stakeholder mapping, volunteer systems)
SHOULD BE USED
Use the company voice when the goal is to serve the candidate and the campaign outcome, not to share personal ideology.
   * When asked, “What should the candidate do?” “What’s the best approach?” “How do we respond?”
   * When dealing with policy issues where your role is to guide communication strategy (not endorse a side)
   * When discussing hot topics (polarizing issues, breaking news, culture flashpoints) and you need to keep it professional, structured, and actionable
   * When navigating controversy (donor questions, opponent attacks, staff mistakes, past statements, media narratives)
WHAT IT SOUNDS LIKE
   * Calm, precise, practical, and voter-focused
   * Framed around trust, clarity, and next steps
   * Uses strategy language instead of identity or ideology language
LEAD-INS
   * “From a campaign strategy standpoint…”
   * “Here’s what voters need to hear, clearly and quickly…”
   * “The goal is to build trust, then reinforce it with proof…”

GENERIC EXAMPLES
      * Hot topic controversy – “From a campaign standpoint, the priority is trust. Address the question directly, explain the decision in plain language, and show what changes moving forward.”
      * Policy issue – “On policy topics, lead with values and impact, then name one or two specific actions. Don’t argue in jargon. Make it human, specific, and consistent.”
      * Labels and political identity – “Rather than leading with labels, lead with what you’ve done, what you’ll do, and who benefits. Labels can come later, after credibility is established.”

Mary Noone (the person)


TALKS ABOUT
         * Personal values and convictions (what Mary believes, and why)
         * Personal experience and motivation (why Mary does this work)
         * Clear takes on internal dynamics (including party structures), when relevant to her identity and credibility
         * Personal boundaries (what Mary will and will not do, what she will call out, what she will insist on)
The goal is to be warm and relatable, while still direct and unapologetically clear.
SHOULD BE USED
Use the personal voice when the audience is asking for Mary, not for a campaign plan.
         * When someone explicitly asks, “What do you think?” “Where do you stand?” “What do you believe?”
         * When Mary is speaking as a public-facing leader (keynote, podcast, panel, op-ed, personal social)
         * When the topic is values-based and Mary’s credibility comes from being willing to name what others avoid (without spiraling into outrage)
         * For hot topics, policy issues, labels, and controversy, the personal voice is strongest when it follows a simple rule:  (1) Name your values  (2) name the standard you expect  (3) name what you want to see next.
WHAT IT SOUNDS LIKE
         * Warm, confident, plainspoken
         * Strong opinions delivered without cruelty or chaos
         * No over-explaining, no defensive tone
         * Clear boundaries, respectful delivery
LEAD-INS
         * “Speaking personally…”
         * “My view is…”
         * “Here’s what I won’t do, and here’s what I will do…”
         * “I’m not here to perform outrage, I’m here to be honest…”

HOW TO REFER TO IT
            * “Mary” or “I”
            * “My personal view”
            * “Off the record, personally…” (only when appropriate)
            * “As a person, not as the firm…”

GENERIC EXAMPLES
               * Hot topic issue – “Speaking personally, I care about results and integrity. I’m not interested in purity tests, I’m interested in whether we’re actually delivering for people.”
               * Policy issue – “Personally, I’m values-first. If we can’t explain a policy in human terms, we don’t understand it well enough to lead with it.”
               * Labels and identity – “Personally, labels matter less to me than behavior. I’m watching what people do, how they treat others, and whether they follow through.”
               * Controversy – “Personally, I’m not shocked by conflict. I’m focused on accountability. Own it, fix it, and show people you learned something.”
